FROM THE POSTER TO THE PLANE

A little shift.
An entirely new pattern.

de Bruijn’s pentagrid construction connects a simple system of lines to the rich geometry of Penrose tilings.

Read the original research poster ↗
  1. 01

    Lay down five grids

    Each family is perpendicular to vⱼ = (cos 2πj/5, sin 2πj/5). Its shift γⱼ moves the lines. We keep the five shifts summing to zero.

    x · vⱼ + γⱼ ∈ ℤ
  2. 02

    Find the crossings

    Two grid lines meet at a point. The four adjacent cells supply integer coordinates in five dimensions.

    Kⱼ(x) = ⌈x · vⱼ + γⱼ⌉
  3. 03

    Project into a rhombus

    Map those coordinates back to the plane. Each crossing becomes a thin or thick rhombus, with edges of equal length.

    f(K) = Σ Kⱼvⱼ

A note on aperiodicity: finite patches recur, but no nonzero translation preserves the whole infinite tiling. This explorer shows a finite patch; its tile ratio need not equal φ exactly.
